# FreeDuet roadmap

This page lists what is planned, in the order we currently think it matters. **Nothing here is promised with a date.** A feature is called available only after it runs and is tested ([README: what is tested](../README.md#what-is-tested-and-what-is-not)).

## Where FreeDuet is today

- **Version:** v0.2.2, prepared on 16 November 2026 and not yet published (the maintainer publishes it). The last published version is v0.2.1 of 15 November 2026 at commit `ddb0cde`. Before it, v0.2 was published as a release on 14 November 2026 at commit `e2e600d` (tag `v0.2`), and v0.1 of 13 November 2026 was an early release, built and used by one maintainer. **Nothing in v0.2 has been run live on a real installation.**
- **Run live on one installation, 15 November 2026:** on the maintainer's own installation (Windows with WSL2, Gmail through an app password), the update was run four times, each time with a database backup taken first; the move from no sign-in to Google sign-in ran (1,699 rows moved, backup taken); and Otto moved spam to the Spam folder on a real mailbox, ordered through Iris, with no card. The other items of v0.2.1 are not yet run live on a real installation.
- **Run live on one installation, 16 November 2026:** on the maintainer's own installation, a standing mail rule for one sender domain was created from the person's typed order with no card, applied to the mail already there, and applied by itself to new mail at the next pass; Iris handed a "from now on" mail request to Otto (PR #279); and the notice for a browser that is not paired was seen on a phone. Only that one mail rule has run live, so the other rules and the eval cases otto-16 and otto-17 have not. Not run live: the card allowed inside a hand-off (D48), and the members' sentences about why a card appears (D47), which have not been observed with a model.
- **What v0.2.2 adds to v0.2.1:** standing mail rules that FreeDuet applies to new mail by itself, for five actions only (label, archive, mark read, move to Spam or move to the Bin) (D46, PR #274); Iris passing a "from now on" mail request to Otto (PR #279); a notice when the browser is not paired, and members told why a card appears (D47, PR #278); a card allowed inside a hand-off runs and its answer comes home, with no second card (D48, PR #279); the threat model, the verify guide and the changelog (PR #270); dependency alerts closed in PRs #272 and #277; and corrected documents (PRs #273, #275, #276).
- **What v0.2.1 adds to v0.2:** a one-command update that copies your data first and puts back the version and the data if the new version does not answer; a "done when" line on every hand-off, checked against the answer; one line with Undo after a team member is added from chat; a routine's card in the chat, with Pause, Edit and Cancel; a team proposed at your first message; Otto can create labels, archive, mark read or unread, and move mail to the Bin and to Spam, with no card when your own written order names the change and the messages, and a card otherwise; connecting a Gmail mailbox from the chat in one line and one button; a member's name, tone and language changed in chat, each with a card and Undo, and a setting changed only in your own live chat turn; a team, or one member, exported as one file (importing one into the app is not built yet), and the 8,000-character limit for an imported member's instructions; a routine's history kept for 90 days, with a "Missed" label and a link from each run to its conversation; a remote session with limited powers, and the tool that moves what you made with no sign-in to the address you sign in with; a message from the microphone that always asks with the card; a preview, which saves nothing, of what an import from another tool would keep; approving a tool from the inbox never grants a send tool; the desktop app makes its own database password.
- **What v0.2 adds to v0.1:** each installation makes its own secrets, and each member's computer has its own token; with no sign-in only this machine's own names are answered; member computers cannot reach your home or office network unless you switch that on; a tool nobody classified asks first, and routines always ask; a tool the person runs replaces the published vault key; an Engines page that shows each member's chain and the automatic or ask-first choice; a second engine kind, off by default, and a spend ledger with caps; two new members, Mark and Cam; only Otto sends email; "Full access without payments" as a second setting; a Download button for files a member makes; settings changed in chat, with Undo; routines with Next runs and History; first-step install scripts for Ubuntu and for Windows through WSL2; sign-in limited to exact email addresses; read-aloud with a local voice, off by default.
- **Team:** twelve members. Iris is the Chief of Staff; the others each have one job ([TEAM.md](TEAM.md)).
- **Engine:** your own Claude subscription, through the unmodified Claude Code login. No API key.
- **Where it runs:** Windows with WSL2, and Linux. macOS has not been tested.
- **What you need:** Docker, Bash and Bun. On Ubuntu 22.04 and 24.04 a first install script ([install.sh](../openbot/scripts/install.sh)) sets them up, but it has not yet run on a clean machine. On Windows a first script ([install.ps1](../openbot/scripts/install.ps1)) does the same through WSL2, and it has never run on a real Windows machine; on any other system you set them up by hand.
- **Tests:** the team evals were certified at v0.1 (each member at 100% of its scored cases, safety cases 45 of 45 runs). One full live run on v0.2.1, on 15 November 2026, covered 84 cases, 38 of them safety cases: 66 of 73 scored cases passed and 11 were skipped, because they need Gmail, routines or settings, which the eval stack does not have connected. That run did not meet the thresholds (each member at least 90%, safety cases 100%). Each of its seven failures was re-run three times: penny-5 passed 3 of 3; iris-1 and cam-6 passed 2 of 3; iris-16, iris-22, jules-6 and cam-7 failed every time (all four were added after the v0.1 certification). The four were fixed in PR #269 without lowering any safety requirement, and then passed 12 of 12 runs. A full three-run certification has not been repeated on v0.2.1, and the public report of the 15 November run is not published yet. The last full certification is still v0.1's.

## How to read the tables

One rule for every page of this project (the README, the comparison, the site): a green tick ✅ means you can use it today, in a published version. Something being built is marked 🚧, something decided and not started 🗓️. Decided 15 November 2026.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Now** | The next things to be worked on. |
| **Next** | After that. Several of these can start once the first group is done. |
| **Later** | Wanted, but waiting on a decision, a design or something in Now or Next. |
| **Planned** | We want it and have not started designing it. |
| **In design** | The shape is being worked out; options are still open. |
| **In progress** | Work has started. |

The order inside a group is not a ranking, except that the one-file installer is the top priority overall: it is today's biggest barrier.

## What will not change

These hold whatever else on this page moves.

- **Logins first.** Members run on the subscriptions you signed in to. A metered API key or API credit is never first, never on by default, and always capped ([PROVIDERS.md](PROVIDERS.md)).
- **Your approval for real account actions, by default.** Payments, passwords and security settings always come to you, by a fixed rule in the code. Every other send, signature, booking, publication or deletion is checked by a reviewer model against what you typed, and comes to you unless it is clearly what you asked. There are three exceptions, and all are yours: a new email to an address you typed yourself in your own message (D27 in [M1_PLAN.md](M1_PLAN.md)); a standing mail rule you set in your own words, which runs by itself and can only label, archive, mark read, or move mail to Spam or the Bin (D46); and "Full access", which only you can turn on, member by member, after a warning, in one of two settings: with payments, or without them, so that payments and purchases still ask ([FULL_ACCESS.md](FULL_ACCESS.md)). Full access lifts only the payments rule. Passwords, credentials and security settings stay with you either way.
- **A spoken yes never approves.** An approval is always a press on the card. Voice can read the card out, but it never approves.
- **Routines always ask.** A routine or trigger that writes, and any change to its setup, always shows you the approval card. Full access does not unlock this. The one exception is a standing mail rule you set in your own words (D46).
- **No secrets taken, no telemetry by default.** FreeDuet never reads, stores or forwards your subscription login. FreeDuet sends no usage data to its maintainers, and the runtime's own telemetry is off in `.env.example` (`COPILOTKIT_TELEMETRY_DISABLED=true`). Optional CopilotKit Intelligence is off by default too ([telemetry.md](telemetry.md), [PROVIDERS.md](PROVIDERS.md)).
- **Self-hosted, and your data stays with you.** Your conversations and memory stay in your own database.
- **MIT.** One free licence for everyone, with no paid-only or enterprise part. A few ported files keep their original notices ([NOTICE.md](../NOTICE.md)).
- **We say what is tested and what is not.** Planned means planned.

Want something moved up? Open an issue and say what you need it for.
