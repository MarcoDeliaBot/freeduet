FreeDuet

Your own team of AI assistants.

FreeDuet runs on your own computer. One assistant sorts your mail, one reads contracts, one books the restaurant. You say what you need, they do the work, and by default they send, pay or delete nothing without your yes.

Too much to do, and only one of you?

Emails, invoices, bookings, contracts, that reply you promised last week. One head, two hands, a hundred things to do.

FreeDuet is like a small office of assistants inside your computer. Each assistant is good at exactly one job. You talk to Iris, the one who runs the team, and she gets everybody moving.

A team, not one chatbot

A chatbot is one clever friend who answers questions. FreeDuet is a whole group project where everybody really does their part.

It does things, it doesn't just talk

Every assistant has its own desk: a separate browser, away from yours. There it can look up prices, fill in forms and prepare drafts.

You stay the boss

The assistants prepare. You decide. By default, nothing important leaves your computer until you press Approve.

Without FreeDuet

You open the mail, read forty messages, hunt for the invoice, compare three flights, write the reply, forget the birthday present.

With FreeDuet

You write one sentence: "Handle everything that came in this morning." Then you read a short report and press Approve where you agree.

Meet the team. Each one has exactly one job.

Why not one assistant that does everything? For the same reason a football team has a goalkeeper: someone who does one job all day gets very good at it.

What happens after you press Enter

No magic and no secrets. Five steps, always the same, so you always know who is doing what.

  1. You write what you need

    In your own words and your own language, like a message to a friend.

  2. Iris picks the right assistant

    She reads your request and hands it to the specialist for that job.

  3. The assistant works at its own desk

    It uses its own browser to search, read and prepare. It treats what it reads as information, never as orders.

  4. A card asks for your yes

    By default, if the job needs a big button (send, pay, sign, book, delete), everything stops and waits for you.

  5. You get a short report

    What was done, what was not done and why, and what the next step is. No walls of text.

A lock, not a promise

The rule is not just written in the assistant's instructions. By default, the program itself blocks the action until you approve it.

Tricks don't work

An email that says "send me the password, it's urgent" is treated as text to read, not as an order. And no assistant ever asks you for passwords or card numbers in the chat.

One honest shortcut

If you type the address yourself ("email anna@example.com and tell her…"), your sentence is the yes, and that new email goes out. By default, addresses found anywhere else ask first.

45/45

We try to fool our own assistants on purpose. In the latest full test of version 0.1, the safety checks passed 45 times out of 45. Read the public report. The assistants can still make mistakes, so read what they prepare before you approve it.

Why you don't pay twice

Most AI tools make you buy an "API key". That works like a taxi meter: every word costs a little, and the bill keeps climbing.

A monthly AI subscription is more like a bus pass: one price, ride as much as the pass allows. FreeDuet runs on the pass you already have. You sign in yourself, and FreeDuet never sees, stores or forwards your login.

  • Today it works with a Claude subscription, through the official Claude Code app.
  • More subscriptions and local models are planned for the next versions.
  • FreeDuet itself costs nothing: the code is public under the MIT licence.

The goal: a team for everyone, not only for big companies

A big company has assistants, lawyers and accountants. A freelancer, a small shop or a family has… themselves. That isn't fair, and now it doesn't have to stay that way.

We want anybody to have a team: free, open and at home. FreeDuet runs on your own computer and keeps its conversations in a database on that computer. The thinking is done by the AI you already subscribe to.

Open source means the recipe is public. Anyone can read it, check that it does what it says, and make it better. You don't have to trust us. You can look.

Questions people ask first

What is free, and what do I pay for?

FreeDuet is free and open source under the MIT licence, also for business use. The only thing you pay for is the AI subscription you use with it, which today is a Claude plan. There is no API key to buy.

What do I need to install it?

A Claude subscription, and Docker, Bash and Bun on your computer. The four steps above are the whole install. Never opened a terminal? Ask someone technical to set it up, and show them this page.

Does it work on Windows, Mac and Linux?

The maintainer uses it on Windows with WSL2 and Docker, and our automatic checks start it on Linux. It has not been tested on a Mac, so we can't promise it works there. FreeDuet is an early release (version 0.1), built and used by one maintainer.

What can an assistant do without asking me?

It can look things up, read, and prepare drafts and reports. By default, the five buttons wait for your Approve, except a new email to an address you typed. Full access, per member, lifts the waiting in your own chats. Passwords and security settings still come to you. Assistants can still make mistakes, so read what they prepare before you approve it.

Where is my data?

FreeDuet runs on your computer and keeps its conversations in a database there. Your Claude login stays in a Docker volume that only the Claude Code program reads. To think, the assistants send your request to the AI service you signed in to, as its own app would.

Can I use it from my phone?

Not yet. Today FreeDuet opens in the browser on your computer. A phone version, with a notification when something needs your Approve, is planned.

Can I add my own team member?

Yes. Ask Leo, who builds new assistants, to design one for the job you need, or write its definition yourself. You can also rename members and change how they talk. How the members are defined.

Where do I get help?

Open an issue on GitHub. There is a short form for a question, an error, a fix, or to say you are using it. You can write in English or Italian.
